The Holstentor is a late-Gothic city gate built in 1478, and the most recognisable symbol of Lübeck, Germany — once the 'Queen of the Hanse'. With its two round towers and arched central passage, the gate guarded the western approach to the city across the river Trave. Lübeck was the capital of the Hanseatic League for over three centuries, and the Holstentor's imposing brick facade still bears the Latin inscription 'Concordia Domi Foris Pax' — Harmony Within, Peace Without.
